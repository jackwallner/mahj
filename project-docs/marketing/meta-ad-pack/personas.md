# Buyer personas for the Meta ad pack

Built from `research.md`: competitor App Store reviews, public Facebook posts,
the long-running niche ads, and Mahj Trainer's own four reviews. Each one is a
composite, not a real person. Their words are paraphrased from what players
wrote. Use them to score every ad in `scoring.md`, adversarially: each persona
is busy, skeptical and one thumb-flick from the next post.

## P1 Linda, 58, brand-new and just invited

- **Situation:** Retired school administrator in suburban Atlanta. Her
  neighbour's group lost a fourth and asked her in. She took one 101 class at
  the community centre six weeks ago and has played three times. Still sorts
  her rack slowly and counts tiles twice.
- **In her words:** "I went to the class and felt completely lost. Everyone
  else seems to know the card by heart. My biggest worry is holding everyone
  up in the Charleston."
- **Stops scrolling for:** a rack that looks like hers with "American Mah
  Jongg" on it; anything that names the Charleston fear without making her
  feel stupid; big readable type.
- **Distrusts:** ads that look like mahjong solitaire, "seniors" language,
  slick stock models half her age, anything that smells like a subscription
  trap ("free" with fine print).
- **Installs if:** it promises she can practise alone, privately, in a few
  minutes, and the first screen looks easy. **Doesn't if:** it looks like a
  full game against bots (that is what scared her at the table).

## P2 Barbara, 67, returning after 30 years

- **Situation:** Played with her mother's friends in New Jersey in the 1980s,
  stopped when the kids came. Now widowed and retired in Scottsdale, her
  clubhouse has three mahj tables. She knows the tiles but the card has changed
  and she is rusty on strategy.
- **In her words:** "I used to play all the time. The card is totally
  different now and I don't want to look like a beginner."
- **Stops scrolling for:** something that respects that she already knows the
  game; "the card changes every April" as a known truth; clean, classy design.
- **Distrusts:** baby-step beginner copy ("What is a tile?"), cartoon
  graphics, anything claiming to teach "the 2026 card" (she knows that's the
  League's), emoji-heavy copy.
- **Installs if:** it shows real decisions (which section, what to pass) at a
  level above tile names. **Doesn't if:** it reads like a kids' app or a slot
  machine.

## P3 Carol, 52, intermediate and frustrated by the new card

- **Situation:** Works full time in Columbus, plays every Tuesday night with
  the same five women for four years. Wins sometimes. Every April she feels
  set back when the new card arrives, and she freezes when she has to pivot.
- **In her words:** "Every spring I'm a beginner again. I can play, I just
  can't see which hand to switch to fast enough."
- **Stops scrolling for:** a specific skill problem (pivoting, reading a rack
  in seconds), a challenge she can test herself on in the feed.
- **Distrusts:** vague "learn mahjong" promises (she already knows how),
  testimonials that sound invented, anything that would put the actual card's
  hands on screen (she knows that's not allowed and it reads as shady).
- **Installs if:** the ad shows a drill harder than she expects and fast to
  answer on a lunch break. **Doesn't if:** it is clearly only for beginners.

## P4 Debbie, 63, club organizer who teaches

- **Situation:** Runs a 30-member mahj club at her synagogue in Boca Raton,
  teaches four-week beginner classes twice a year. Gets asked weekly "how do I
  practise between lessons?" Wary of tools that teach wrong rules.
- **In her words:** "My new players don't practise between classes, then they
  forget the suits and whose turn it is. I need something I can recommend
  without having to correct it."
- **Stops scrolling for:** accuracy cues, original teaching material, a tool
  she could send to her students; "not affiliated with the NMJL" reassures her.
- **Distrusts:** rule mistakes (she will spot one in the ad and screenshot it
  to the group chat), card hands reproduced in an ad, "win every game" hype.
- **Installs if:** the ad teaches one correct thing in a way she would teach it.
  **Doesn't if:** one rule on screen is wrong or oversimplified.

## P5 Joan, 71, grandmother who wants a seat at the family table

- **Situation:** Lives near her daughter in Charlotte. Her daughter and
  granddaughter (24) picked up mahj in the recent boom and play with friends;
  they invited Joan to a family game night at Thanksgiving. Uses Facebook
  daily, iPad more than phone, reading glasses.
- **In her words:** "My daughter taught me the basics. At my age I wasn't sure
  I could still learn something new, but I don't want to slow them down."
- **Stops scrolling for:** warmth, family, big type, very simple first step;
  being told this is easy to start, not that she is old.
- **Distrusts:** small text, busy screenshots, words like "senior" or "brain
  games", anything that needs an account or a card number before she sees it.
- **Installs if:** she can read every word without zooming and it says "free
  to start" plainly. **Doesn't if:** the screen looks crowded or technical.
