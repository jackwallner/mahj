## What survived, ranked

1. **SM-01** (CONFIRMED): a lapsed member's game night reminder outlives the membership and cannot be turned off in the app. Needs more than 3 days between the last open and the first open after the lapse, which is the common case.
2. **SM-03** (CONFIRMED-MINOR): weekly gain counts regained Mahj+ access as learning. Known since 2026-10-16.
3. **SM-02** (CONFIRMED-MINOR): reminder toggles do not reconcile with iOS permission or pending requests at launch. The revoke route is certain from the code and user-caused; the restore route is unverified on the OS side. To settle the restore half: on a device, enable the daily reminder, back up to iCloud, restore to another phone, then compare `pendingNotificationRequests()` with the toggle.

## "Considered and not claimed"

Each was checked; none is a missed bug worth promoting.

- Remote switches ON while `offerings` is nil: first-ever offline launch only. Agree.
- `loadOfferings` nils `offerings` after a failure: agree, callers reload only when the package is missing.
- Saved runs resume against current content: agree it is latent. `QuizDrillView.swift:10-12` restores the saved `index` and `QuizDrillView.swift:37` indexes the library's `questions`, so a release that shortens a drill would crash on resume. Worth a guard the next time content shrinks.
- Trial-end reminder dead code, permission callback race, churn `spent` flag, streak across time zones: agree.

Seen while checking, not claimed and not hunted further:

- Same root as SM-01, other direction (UNVERIFIED): a member whose period renewed while the app was closed more than 3 days reads as a resolved non-member from the cache until the network answers (`SubscriptionService.swift:285`, RevenueCat's 3-day grace). A cold launch from the game night notification then routes to the paywall (`AppRouter.swift:58-65`) and closes it once the network says member (`HomeView.swift:319-321`); offline it stays. That contradicts the `membershipResolved` comment at `SubscriptionService.swift:210-213`. Settle with a sandbox member, app closed over a renewal for 4 days, launched from the notification.
- The teardown at `HomeView.swift:325-327` also runs on a temporary lapse (billing retry with no grace period) and never turns the reminder back on when the member recovers. A product call.

## Method changes

The three false positives share one cause: the invariant was written by the auditor, then the code was judged against it. All three behaviours were documented, and two were asserted by passing tests in the repo. Changes, in order of how many errors each would have caught here:

1. **Search the repo for intent before claiming.** Grep the existing tests, `.claude/rules/` and the comments at the cited lines for the behaviour. A passing test that asserts it (SM-05, SM-06) or a rules paragraph that describes it means NOT A BUG; at most a product question. Would have removed 2 of 3 false positives.
2. **Every invariant must quote its source** (UI copy, doc comment, AGENTS.md, rules file, What's New) with file:line. An invariant with no source cannot produce a bug, only a question. I-23, I-27 and I-28 had none and produced SM-05, SM-04 and SM-06.
3. **Keep product calls out of the bug count and the ranking.** List them under "Questions for Jack" with the trade-off. The audit's headline "6 bugs" counted three it had itself labelled product calls.
4. **Trace the proposed fix end to end before claiming.** SM-04's fix (keep the token across Reset) would rank the player on their pre-reset score because the server keeps `greatest()`. If the fix creates a different mismatch, the current behaviour may be the deliberate choice.
5. **A failing test is proof only of what it asserts.** Each `testBug...` must name the promise line its expected value comes from. A store-level test proves the store's behaviour, not reachability: pair it with the tap route and the view guards checked (which buttons render for that membership state). SM-04 and SM-05 were "proved by test" and are not bugs.
6. **For launch, cache and entitlement claims, read the pinned SDK's source.** SM-01 is real, but its route ("let the subscription end while the app is closed") does not reproduce if the tester relaunches within 3 days, because RevenueCat 5.72 judges a cached record against its request date for 3 days. A tester following the audit's route could wrongly close a real bug.
7. **State severity from what the OS actually does.** "Nothing ever fires" (SM-02) describes a state the player chose; say which part the app misstates and what happens when the player reverses it.
