The rewrite is written so it is correct the day the territories open. Jack decides whether to fix the trader status; it needs the legal and contact details, so I did not touch it.

## How the storefronts index these locales

From Apple's table (developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-store-localizations, fetched 2026-11-20):

- Germany, Austria: German, plus English (U.K.). Switzerland: German, English (U.K.), French, Italian. Luxembourg: English (U.K.), French, German.
- Italy: Italian, English (U.K.).
- Netherlands: Dutch, English (U.K.). Belgium: English (U.K.), Dutch, French.
- Portugal: Portuguese (Portugal), English (U.K.).
- Spain: Spanish (Spain), Catalan, English (U.K.). So es-ES, ca and en-GB are one keyword pool for Spain.
- United Kingdom, Ireland: English (U.K.) only.
- Australia, New Zealand: English (Australia) plus English (U.K.). So en-AU and en-GB are one pool for both.
- Mexico and Brazil: their own language plus English (U.K.). The US does not index en-GB, es-ES, de-DE, it, nl-NL, pt-PT or ca.

Design rules that follow:

- The US is 96% of proceeds but none of these eight locales is a US field, so the aim is the local storefront and the cross-checks below. No US-only repetition rule applies, so en-GB may reuse en-US words.
- Keyword tokens are not repeated inside any storefront pool. A script (`/tmp`, not committed) checked every pool: Spain (es-ES, ca, en-GB), Australia and New Zealand (en-AU, en-GB), Switzerland (de-DE, it, en-GB), Germany and Austria, Italy, Netherlands, Portugal. Clean after two fixes: `estratègia` (ca) normalizes to es-ES `estrategia`, and `majong` (de-DE) was already in en-GB.
- Because en-GB is indexed as a secondary field in all of those storefronts, the English terms (tutorial, coach, quiz, joker, rack, nmjl) live once in en-GB and are not repeated in German, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese or Spanish fields.
- en-GB repeats `quiz` and `rack` against es-MX in Mexico (both indexed there). Kept: the UK has no other source for them.
- ca's keyword field is the second Spanish pool in Spain, so it carries Catalan words plus Spanish synonyms es-ES does not (`lecciones,consejos,normas,jugar`). Most Catalan speakers search in Spanish too.

## Method and limits

- Autocomplete: `MZSearchHints` for DE 143443, GB 143444, AU 143460, IT 143450, NL 143452, PT 143453, ES 143454 (also used for ca). 15 to 40 seeds each, English plus the local language.
- Competitors: iTunes Search API titles and ratings; subtitles read from the App Store product page (`<p class="subtitle">`).
- Ranks: Search API, 200 results, our app 6790052126. Only GB and AU can show a rank; elsewhere the app is absent.
- Autocomplete in these markets is thin and almost entirely solitaire (`mahjong kostenlos`, `mahjong gratis`, `mahjong blast`). Nothing came back for `regeln`, `regole`, `regels`, `reglas`, `regras`, `principianti`, `beginners`, `tutorial`, `anfänger`. An empty hint is not evidence of no demand. Catalan returned no hint for any seed, so `ca` is thin evidence.
- Astro was not used (not available on this machine). No popularity scores; picks rest on autocomplete, competitor gaps and result-set size.
- Competitor names appear only in the evidence below as titles read from the store, never in a field.

What the research did show, and drove the picks: in every EU language the only learning apps are Riichi apps with 0 to 1 ratings ("Mahjong & Mahjong: Lernen", "Mahjong Mind", "Mahjong Master", "PVMahjong", "RealMahjong"), and the result sets for the learning queries are tiny (`mahjong regels` 8, `mahjong regole` 5, `mahjong principianti` 3, `mahjong corso` 2, `mahjong principiantes` 4, `mahjong reglas` 6, `mahjong iniciantes` 1, `mahjong amerikaans` 2, `mahjong übung` 1). Nobody targets American mahjong in German, Italian, Dutch or Portuguese, except one Italian and one Spanish-market app (see it, es-ES). Ranking should be reachable once the storefronts are open.
