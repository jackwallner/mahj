## Jokers in American Mah Jongg

Last updated October 21, 2026

A joker is a wild tile that stands in for any tile inside a group of three or more, and a set has eight of them, with strict rules. Most beginner mistakes at the table are joker mistakes, and every one of them is avoidable.

## What can a joker do?

A joker stands in for any tile inside a group of three or more: a pung, a kong, a quint or bigger. A kong is often two natural tiles and two jokers. A quint needs at least one joker by definition, because only four of any tile exist.

7D7D7DJJK

That kong of 7 Dots is legal: three naturals and a joker.

## What can a joker never do?

A joker can never sit in a pair, be a single, appear in a Singles and Pairs hand, be passed in the Charleston, or be called after it is discarded.

- **Sit in a pair.** A pair is two real tiles. Always.
- **Be a single.** A hand that calls for a lone tile needs the real tile.
- **Appear in a Singles and Pairs hand.** Those hands have no groups of three, so there is nowhere a joker may sit. That is why they pay the most.
- **Be passed in the Charleston** or the courtesy pass.
- **Be called after it is discarded.** A thrown joker is dead for everyone.

## How does a joker swap work?

If an exposed group on anyone's rack, including your own, contains a joker, and you hold the real tile it is standing for, you may exchange them on your turn: hand over the real tile, take the joker into your hand. It happens after you draw and before you discard. Once you have thrown, the window closes until your next turn.

This is why good players scan every exposure every turn. A joker sitting in someone else's kong of 5 Bams is yours for the price of a 5 Bam you did not need anyway.

The swap is also the one change an exposure can take after it is locked. Once its owner has discarded, an exposed pung never grows into a kong; the fourth tile stays on the rack or goes to the discards.

## How do you count jokers?

Subtract the jokers you can see on racks and in the discards from the eight in the set. If you can see six on racks and in the discards, only two are left anywhere, and a quint hand is probably a wish. Counting jokers is the first real "reading the table" skill, and it is cheap: jokers are loud.

## Should you ever discard a joker?

Almost never. A discarded joker is dead, so it is a safe throw in the narrow sense that nobody can call it, but you are giving up the most flexible tile in the set and telling the table you had one to spare. Throw one only when your hand is complete without it and nothing else is safe.

## Common questions

### Can a joker be used in a pair?

No. Pairs and singles must always be the real tiles. Jokers only work in groups of three or more.

### How many jokers are in a set?

Eight.

### Can I take a joker from an opponent's exposure?

Yes, if you hold the real tile it stands for. Hand over the real tile on your turn, before you discard, and take the joker.

### What happens if someone discards a joker?

It is dead. Nobody can call it, for an exposure or for mah jongg.

### Can jokers be used in a Singles and Pairs hand?

Never. Those hands have no groups of three or more, so there is nowhere a joker can legally sit.

### Why would you ever discard a joker in mahjong?

Rarely, and only for defense. A discarded joker is dead, so nobody can call it, which makes it a safe throw when every other tile in your hand could feed someone. Do it only when your hand is complete without the joker and nothing else is safe, because you give up the most flexible tile in the set.

### What can you not do with a joker in mahjong?

A joker cannot sit in a pair or stand in as a single, and it cannot appear anywhere in a Singles and Pairs hand. You cannot pass one in the Charleston or the courtesy pass, and nobody can call a joker after it is discarded.
