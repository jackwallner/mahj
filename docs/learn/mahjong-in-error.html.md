# What Happens If You Call Mahjong Wrong?

## What happens if you call mahjong wrong?

Last updated November 12, 2026

If you call mah jongg by mistake and catch it before you lay any tiles down, take the call back and play goes on with no penalty. Once you have exposed your hand and it does not match a line on the card, it is dead: you stop playing for the rest of that hand and still pay whoever wins.

## The rule, step by step

1. **You say "mah jongg."** Saying the word alone costs nothing if you catch the mistake before your tiles go face up. Put back any discard you took and play continues.
2. **You lay the hand down.** The table checks it against the card. If it matches, you win.
3. **It does not match.** Your hand is dead. Leave it alone; you stop drawing, discarding and calling for the rest of the hand.
4. **Play goes on.** The turn passes to the player on your right, and the other three keep going.
5. **Someone wins.** You pay them like everyone else. If the wall runs out instead, nobody pays.

## What counts as a wrong mah jongg?

Any hand that does not exactly match one line on the current card. The common ones:

- The right pattern in the wrong suits. The colors on the card say how many suits a hand uses.
- A concealed (C) hand with exposures on the rack.
- A joker in a pair or a single, or anywhere in a Singles and Pairs hand.
- One tile short or one tile over. A winning hand is exactly 14 tiles.
- Last year's card. A hand from an old card is not a hand this year.

## What if other players threw in their tiles?

A player who tossed in or exposed their own tiles because of your call is out of that hand too, and whoever is left plays on. League rules are stricter about the player who caused it, and home tables vary, so agree on your table's rule before it comes up. The simplest protection is the habit every teacher drills: check the card, then say the word.

## Where tables play it differently

- **Beginner tables** often let a new player pick the tiles back up after a wrong reveal and keep playing. That is a kindness, not the League rule.
- **Tournaments** are strict: exposed and wrong is dead, with no takebacks.
- **Misnamed discards.** If a player names a discard wrong and you claim it for mah jongg on that name, League rules put the cost on the player who misnamed it. Say the name of every tile you throw.

## A worked example

South draws a Red Dragon, sees what looks like a finished hand and says "Mah jongg." Before turning anything over, South checks the card again and spots that the hand needs a pair of Red Dragons, and the rack holds only one. South says so, shows nothing, discards, and play carries on. No harm done.

Two hands later, North calls West's 9 Bam for mah jongg and lays the rack face up. The table sees a kong in Dots where the card line wants Craks:

3D3D3DJJK

North's hand is dead. The 9 Bam stays with North's dead hand, play passes to North's right, and when East wins a few turns later, North pays East along with South and West.

## Common questions

### Can you take back a mahjong call?

Yes, if you have not exposed your tiles yet. Say so right away, return any discard you took, and play continues with no penalty. Once your hand is face up and wrong, it is dead.

### Do you still pay if you call mahjong wrong?

Yes. A dead hand still pays whoever wins that hand. If the wall runs out with no winner, nobody pays.

### Who plays next after a wrong mahjong?

The player on the right of the person who called in error. The dead player is skipped for the rest of the hand.

### Can someone else win on the same discard?

Yes, if they also wanted it for mah jongg. The second claimant takes the tile and wins. If they only wanted it for an exposure, it stays with the dead hand.

### Is calling mahjong in error cheating?

No. It is a mistake, and every player makes it eventually. The rules only take the hand out of play so nobody gains from it.
