## The Charleston, explained

Last updated October 21, 2026

The Charleston is a tile-passing ritual that happens before play begins in American Mah Jongg. It is the part of the game that scares every new player, and the part that practice fixes fastest.

## What is the order of passes in the Charleston?

The first Charleston goes right, across, then left, and an optional second one goes left, across, then right. After the deal and before the first discard, every player passes three tiles at once, three times:

1. **First Right.** Pass three tiles to the player on your right.
2. **First Across.** Pass three to the player opposite.
3. **First Left.** Pass three to the player on your left.

That is the first Charleston, and it is required. A second Charleston happens only if all four players agree. It runs the other way: Left, Across, Right. Then comes the courtesy pass: an optional exchange across the table of anywhere from zero to three tiles, and both players must pass the same number, so ask first.

## What is a blind pass in the Charleston?

A blind pass is sending on tiles you were just handed without looking at them, allowed only on the last pass of each Charleston. Every pass except the last one of each Charleston must come from tiles you have looked at. On the last pass (First Left, and Second Right), you may pass blind: take some or all of the three tiles you were just handed and send them straight on without looking. That is what you do when you genuinely have nothing to spare. Many players call this stealing.

## Can you pass a joker in the Charleston?

**No. Jokers never pass.** Not in any Charleston pass, not blind, not in the courtesy pass. Passing a joker is illegal.

## How do you choose three tiles to pass?

Pass the isolated tiles that fit no family first, and let the hand show itself over the next two passes. New players freeze because they try to decide on a hand before the first pass. Do not. The first pass is for clearing junk, and the hand reveals itself over the next two.

### First Right: dump the isolated tiles

Look for tiles that fit no family at all. A lone wind when everything else is numbers. A single 9 when the rack is low. A third suit when two are already crowded. The first pass is almost never a hard decision; if it is, you are overthinking it.

### First Across: pick a direction

By now you have seen a few incoming tiles. Ask the three questions from the [card sections](https://jackwallner.com/ios/mahj/learn/card-sections.html) page: odd, even or mixed? One number repeating? Honors piling up? Pass against whatever you are leaning toward. Leaning evens? Odds go.

### First Left: commit or pass blind

If your rack now reads as one section, pass the last three tiles that do not belong to it. If you are still torn between two sections, keep the tiles both sections share and pass the rest. If nothing can go, pass blind.

## Which tiles should you keep even when they look like junk?

Keep flowers, pairs and soaps even when they look like junk.

- **Flowers.** Many hands want two, three or four. A flower is rarely dead weight early.
- **Pairs.** A pair is the hardest group to build later, because you cannot call for one. Break a pair last.
- **Soaps.** They do double duty as the zero in year hands and as the dot suit's dragon, so they are contested.

## What does your pass tell other players?

Every pass tells your neighbor something. Three winds in a row says you are not building Winds and Dragons. Three craks says the crak family is open. Good players read incoming passes the way they read exposures. Do not let that paralyze you; just know it is information.

## Common questions

### Is the second Charleston required?

No. The first Charleston (right, across, left) is required. The second (left, across, right) happens only if all four players agree.

### Can I pass fewer than three tiles?

Not in the Charleston itself: every pass is exactly three. Only the courtesy pass across the table is zero to three, by agreement.

### What is a blind pass?

Passing along tiles you were just handed without looking at them. It is allowed only on the last pass of each Charleston: First Left and Second Right.

### Can I pass a joker in the Charleston?

Never. Not in any pass, not blind, not in the courtesy pass.

### Can you call Mah Jongg during the Charleston?

No. During the Charleston players only pass tiles, and nobody draws or discards, so there is nothing to call. Calling starts with East's first discard once the passes are over. The rare exception is a dealer whose original 14 tiles already form a hand on the card: some tables let East declare before passing, but the NMJL rulebook does not clearly cover it, so agree on it as a house rule.

### What should you not pass in the Charleston?

Never pass a joker: it is illegal in every pass, including blind passes and the courtesy pass. Beyond that, think twice before passing flowers (many hands want several), pairs (you cannot call a discard to make one) and soaps (they are both the zero in year hands and the dot suit's dragon). Pass the isolated tiles that fit no family first.
